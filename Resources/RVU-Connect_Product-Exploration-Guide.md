## 01. Introduction
*(Guide Page 03)*

**RVU Connect** is a university-facing digital platform developed by **Iterium** for the **Office of Student Affairs at RV University**.

It is intended to improve the university’s digital experience and make relevant information, services, and interactions more accessible to the student community.

As part of **RE:RVU v1.0**, participants are invited to explore the existing platform and identify opportunities to improve its usability, navigation, visual design, and overall product experience.

> **Core Philosophy:**  
> The objective is **not** to redesign everything or introduce features without purpose.  
> The objective is to **make RVU Connect more useful to the people who use it.**

Your task is to explore the platform as a real user, understand its existing end-to-end experience, identify points of friction, and demonstrate through prototypes how it could be meaningfully improved.

## 02. Access the Platform
*(Guide Page 03)*

* **Live Platform URL:** [`https://rvu-connect.vercel.app/?route=home`](https://rvu-connect.vercel.app/?route=home)

Explore the platform using the available access and onboarding process.

### Critical Evaluation Checkpoints
As you navigate through the application, pay close attention to:
* **Comprehension:** How easily can a user understand the purpose and value of the platform?
* **Information Retrieval:** How quickly can users locate relevant campus information?
* **Navigation Flow:** Does moving through sections feel intuitive and friction-free?
* **Action Completion:** Are important tasks and interactions easy to initiate and complete?
* **Consistency:** Is visual and behavioural consistency maintained across all screens and states?
* **Repeat Utility:** Does the platform offer enough genuine value for students to return regularly?

> **Guiding Principle:**  
> Do not evaluate the platform based solely on surface aesthetics. **Consider the entire user journey.**

## 03. What Should You Explore?
*(Guide Pages 04–05)*

Use this comprehensive five-part exploration framework while auditing RVU Connect:

---

### A. First-Time User Experience
*Imagine that you are landing on RVU Connect for the very first time.*

#### Ask Yourself:
* Is it immediately clear what the platform is for?
* Can a new user easily understand where to begin?
* Are the most relevant and frequently needed sections easy to identify?
* Is the initial interface straightforward or visually overwhelming?
* Does the platform communicate its institutional purpose effectively?

#### Look For:
* Confusing or ambiguous entry points.
* Unclear or jargon-heavy labels.
* Unnecessary visual or interaction complexity.
* Missing orientation context or inadequate onboarding guidance.

---

### B. Navigation and Information Architecture
*Explore how content, links, and sections are organized and structured.*

#### Ask Yourself:
* Can users find what they need without extensive, aimless searching?
* Are related sections and features logically grouped together?
* Are navigation labels, menus, and breadcrumbs intuitive?
* Is it easy to move between disparate areas of the application?
* Does the navigation pattern remain stable and predictable across routes?

#### Look For:
* Hidden or deeply nested navigation pathways.
* Unclear structural hierarchy between primary and secondary sections.
* Duplicated links, dead-ends, or contradictory pathways.
* Poorly organized or fragmented content clusters.

---

### C. User Interface and Visual Design
*Evaluate how the application communicates information visually.*

#### Ask Yourself:
* Is the visual hierarchy clear and structured (headings, body copy, calls to action)?
* Are primary and secondary actions easily distinguishable?
* Is the design system consistent across components, buttons, and cards?
* Are typography scales, whitespace spacing, and grid alignments effective?
* Is information scannable and comfortable to consume?

#### Look For:
* Inconsistent typography styles, line heights, or component states.
* Unclear visual emphasis where primary actions blend into background elements.
* Visual clutter that distracts from core tasks.
* Low-contrast or poorly readable text elements.

---

### D. Existing Features and Workflows
*Audit existing functionality before proposing net-new features.*

#### Ask Yourself:
* Are existing features intuitive to discover and use?
* Can users successfully complete their intended goals without confusion?
* Are there extraneous or redundant steps in common user journeys?
* Is context-sensitive information presented at the right moment?
* Where can existing workflows be simplified or streamlined?

#### Look For:
* Hidden or obscure feature sets that users fail to find.
* Confusing interaction patterns or awkward modal dialogs.
* High-friction multistep processes.
* Incomplete user flows, missing confirmation feedback, or unhandled empty states.

---

### E. Mobile Experience
*Evaluate the responsive experience on small-screen viewports.*

#### Ask Yourself:
* Is the interface comfortable and natural to operate on a smartphone?
* Are key touch targets and primary actions easily reachable with one hand?
* Is typography readable without zooming or horizontal scrolling?
* Do complex desktop tables and cards adapt cleanly to mobile constraints?
* Is mobile navigation convenient and fluid?

#### Look For:
* Horizontal overflow and broken viewport clipping.
* Undersized touch targets that cause mis-clicks.
* Cluttered or unoptimized mobile navigation bars.
* Inconsistent responsive behavior across screen breakpoints.

## 04. Identifying Product Opportunities
*(Guide Page 06)*

Not every observed flaw requires a brand-new feature. Meaningful product improvements stem from diagnosing **why** an experience fails and proposing focused solutions.

### The 5-Step Innovation Framework:

```text
[1. Observe] ──> [2. Understand] ──> [3. Define] ──> [4. Propose] ──> [5. Demonstrate]
```

1. **Observe:**  
   *Identify a concrete point of friction or limitation in the current platform.*  
   *Example:* A student struggles to locate an important administrative service section.
2. **Understand:**  
   *Analyse why the issue occurs and determine who is negatively impacted.*  
   *Example:* The navigation hierarchy is flat and buries administrative links among unrelated content.
3. **Define:**  
   *Synthesize the observation into a clear, actionable problem statement.*  
   *Example:* Students require an intuitive, discoverable categorization system to access key campus services rapidly.
4. **Propose:**  
   *Formulate a specific, targeted product or UX improvement.*  
   *Example:* Reorganize the information architecture into categorized cards and elevate high-frequency workflows to primary navigation.
5. **Demonstrate:**  
   *Build an interactive prototype showcasing how the revised workflow operates.*  
   *Example:* A clickable prototype displaying the redesigned navigation bar, simplified search, and streamlined service access.

> **Golden Rule:**  
> A proposed improvement must directly resolve a verified problem. **Avoid feature bloat and novelty additions that do not serve real user needs.**

## 05. How to Document Your Findings
*(Guide Page 07)*

For every UX opportunity or improvement you propose, structure your documentation using the following standard template:

### Required Documentation Structure

| Item | What to Include |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Current Experience** | What currently happens in the live RVU Connect platform? |
| **Observation** | What specific behaviour, friction, or anomaly did you notice while interacting with it? |
| **Problem** | Why is this difficult, unclear, inefficient, or ineffective for the user? |
| **Affected Users** | Which exact user groups or student segments experience this issue? |
| **Proposed Improvement** | What specific changes (structural, visual, behavioural) are you proposing? |
| **Expected Benefit** | How does this change measurably enhance usability, speed, or satisfaction? |
| **Prototype** | How does your prototype demonstrate and validate this solution? |

---

### Illustrative Example Problem Analysis
*(Note: Provided as an educational template, not an assertion of an existing defect).*

* **Observation:** A user requires multiple exploratory clicks and backtracking to locate an important institutional section.
* **Problem:** The current information hierarchy lacks visual cues and logical groupings, making the section obscure.
* **Affected Users:** All students attempting to access campus announcements and service directories.
* **Proposed Improvement:** Restructure the top-level hierarchy, introduce categorized service hubs, and implement predictive search.
* **Expected Benefit:** Significant reduction in navigation friction, lower task-completion time, and effortless retrieval of critical resources.

## 06. What Kind of Improvements Are We Looking For?
*(Guide Page 08)*

Participants are encouraged to explore opportunities across five core functional domains (illustrative directions, not mandatory checklists):

1. **01 — Usability Improvements:**
   * Streamlining and simplifying complex interaction patterns.
   * Reducing cognitive load across dense informational screens.
   * Eliminating superfluous steps in standard recurring actions.
   * Making core actions immediately visible and contextually relevant.

2. **02 — Navigation Improvements:**
   * Clarifying information architecture and section categorization.
   * Designing clean, predictable navigation bars, sidebars, or bottom bars.
   * Eliminating dead-ends and providing intuitive backward/forward pathways.
   * Grouping related tools, documents, and contacts into cohesive hubs.

3. **03 — Design Improvements:**
   * Establishing consistent component styling and visual rhythm.
   * Improving typography hierarchy, contrast ratios, and line spacing.
   * Refining layouts to make text, tables, and notices effortless to scan.
   * Applying purposeful whitespace to eliminate claustrophobic UI density.

4. **04 — Workflow Improvements:**
   * Accelerating how students complete frequent tasks (e.g., event signup, service queries).
   * Removing repetitive inputs and optimizing form fields.
   * Designing proactive feedback states (loading, confirmation, success, empty states).
   * Connecting fragmented multi-page tasks into unified flows.

5. **05 — Mobile Improvements:**
   * Optimizing responsive component wrapping and touch target dimensions.
   * Refining mobile drawer menus and gesture interactions.
   * Enhancing readability on small viewports and in outdoor ambient lighting.
   * Ensuring critical actions remain within easy thumb-reach zones.

### Institutional Logo Governance
* **Preserve Masterbrand Identity:** The RV University masterbrand must remain the primary institutional marker.
* **Prohibition on Modification:** Never distort, recolour, disassemble, or independently redesign the university logo.
* **Contrast Compliance:** **The gold-coloured logo must never be placed on a white background.** Always ensure high-contrast placement.

*(Guide Page 10)*

For Track 01, submissions must demonstrate tangible, practical improvements to the existing RVU Connect product through two deliverables:

### Deliverable 1 — Solution Deck (PDF)
Your presentation deck must systematically explain:
1. **01 — Target Area:** The specific section or workflow of RVU Connect you explored.

## 09. Important Principles
*(Guide Page 11)*

Keep these five foundational product principles at the center of your work:

1. **01 — Improve Before Expanding:**  
   Prioritise making existing features reliable, accessible, and intuitive before proposing additional, unverified functionality.
2. **02 — Solve Real Problems:**  
   Every proposed adjustment must anchor directly to a verified user problem or friction point.
3. **03 — Think About the Entire Journey:**  
   Focus on what users need to achieve across the complete workflow, rather than polishing isolated, disconnected screens.
4. **04 — Design for Actual Users:**  
   Visual complexity is not quality. The best design is the one that empowers students and staff to achieve their goals with maximum clarity and minimum effort.
5. **05 — Demonstrate, Don’t Just Describe:**  
   Use your interactive prototype to show, rather than tell, how the improved product functions.
